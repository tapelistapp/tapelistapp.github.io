How to work out how much paint you need

1. Add up the length of all the walls (the perimeter) and multiply by the wall height. A 14 x 12 ft room with 8 ft walls has 52 ft of wall, so 416 sq ft.

2. Take off the doors and windows: about 18 sq ft for a 32 x 80 in door and 12 sq ft for a 36 x 48 in window.

3. Multiply by the number of coats and divide by the coverage on the can, usually 350 to 400 sq ft per gallon. Add about 10% for cutting in, the roller tray and touch-ups.

4. Round up to cans the store sells. A gallon can holds 3.66 to 3.79 litres and a quart about 0.9 litres, so two gallons and a quart can be enough where three gallons would leave a lot over.

Questions

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

Most interior paint labels say 350 to 400 sq ft per gallon per coat on a smooth wall. This calculator uses 375 sq ft (9.2 m² per litre), the average of popular labels. Rough or fresh drywall takes more; prime it first (how much primer).

Do I need two coats?

Usually yes: paint makers rate their coverage and colour for two coats. One coat can do when you repaint the same colour over a sound wall.

Is the ceiling paint the same?

Use a flat ceiling paint; it is a separate product, so it is a separate line here. The ceiling area is simply length x width.
