How much drywall tape do I need?

Short answer: about 0.37 ft of tape per square foot of drywall. Add up the board on the walls and the ceiling, multiply by 0.37 and round up to rolls. A 10 x 10 ft room, walls and ceiling: 148 ft, one 250 ft roll.

How to work it out

1. Board area of the walls: the perimeter times the wall height, minus half of each door and window (the sheets go over them and get cut out). A 10 x 10 ft room with 8 ft walls, one door and one window: 320 − 15 = 305 sq ft.

2. Add the ceiling if you hang it: 100 sq ft. Total 405 sq ft.

3. Multiply by about 0.37 ft of tape per sq ft (1.2 m per m², the USG label rate): 148 ft.

4. Round up to rolls. Paper joint tape comes in 250 ft and 500 ft rolls: 148 ft is one 250 ft roll.

Questions

Paper or mesh tape?

Paper tape is the usual choice for joints and inside corners with premixed compound. The rate above is for paper tape.

Do I need corner bead too?

Only on outside corners, a separate item cut to the wall height at each one. A plain rectangular room has none.
